What the figure shows, and the evidence

  • Structure. Active zones sit opposite the openings of the junctional folds. AChRs pack the fold crests at about 10,000 per µm². Folds raise the postsynaptic area 2–8× depending on species, and they are deepest in humans (Slater 2017, Int J Mol Sci 18:2183, open access). Nanoscale AChR stripes line up with the fold openings and the active zones (York & Zheng 2017, eNeuro, open access).
  • Quanta, potentials and safety factor. A single vesicle (quantum) gives a miniature end-plate potential of about 0.5 mV. A nerve impulse releases tens to hundreds of quanta, enough to bring the end plate from rest (about −80 to −90 mV) past threshold. Release in normal adult mammals exceeds the amount needed by a factor of about 3–5 (the safety factor). Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels in the fold depths add to this margin (Wood & Slater 2001, Prog Neurobiol 64:393–429; Wood & Slater 1997, J Physiol 500:165–176).
  • Release machinery. Ca²⁺ entry through P/Q-type channels at active zones triggers SNARE-mediated, synaptotagmin-dependent exocytosis. This is standard textbook material (Purves et al., Neuroscience, chapters on synaptic transmission; Kandel et al., Principles of Neural Science).
  • Receptor and enzyme. The muscle nicotinic AChR is an α₂βδε pentamer (γ in fetal muscle) that needs two ACh molecules to open. AChE is anchored by ColQ in the basal lamina and works close to the diffusion limit (Quinn 1987, Chem Rev 87:955–979).
  • The trace. The membrane-potential curve is representative and not a recording. Its time axis is stretched; the real end-plate potential lasts a few milliseconds. The "AChE inhibited" toggle is a generic teaching switch and names no drug or dose. With the enzyme blocked, ACh stays in the cleft longer, binds repeatedly and prolongs depolarisation.

Simplified. Left out: perisynaptic Schwann cell detail, the Ca²⁺ buffering and K⁺ channels of the terminal, endocytosis pathways, MuSK, agrin and Lrp4 signalling, the T-tubules and sarcoplasmic reticulum, and excitation–contraction coupling. Particle numbers and timing are illustrative.
