# Small intestine: anatomical dossiers

These profiles guide the narration and the figure work in the video. For each structure they give location, extent, borders and relations, peritoneal relations, external and internal features, blood supply, venous, lymphatic and nerve supply, histology, and the clinical anatomy that explains the physiology.

Sources are the user-supplied textbook excerpts, cited by figure number:
- **Gray's**: Drake, Vogl and Mitchell, *Gray's Anatomy for Students*, 4th ed., Ch. 4
- **Junqueira**: Mescher, *Junqueira's Basic Histology*, 13th ed., Ch. 1–5 and 15–16
- **Guyton**: Hall, *Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology*, 14th ed., Ch. 2, 4, 8, 61 and 63–67

Where these books are silent, standard anatomical teaching fills in the detail.

## 0. Location anatomy: where the small intestine lives

**Abdominal cavity.** The abdominal cavity is bounded above by the diaphragm, which is the inferior thoracic aperture closed by the domes. Below, it is continuous through the pelvic inlet with the pelvic cavity. Its walls are:
- anterolaterally, the muscular abdominal wall;
- posteriorly, the lumbar vertebrae, psoas and quadratus lumborum.

Figures: Gray's 4.1, 4.21.

**Surface planes.**
- The **transpyloric plane (LI)** lies halfway between the jugular notch and the pubic symphysis. It passes through:
  - the pylorus;
  - the 1st part of the duodenum;
  - the neck of the pancreas;
  - the origin of the SMA;
  - the hila of the kidneys;
  - the fundus of the gallbladder.
- The **subcostal** and **intertubercular** planes, crossed by the two **midclavicular** planes, create **nine regions**.
- The **transumbilical** and **median** planes create **four quadrants**.

Figures: Gray's 4.16, 4.22, 4.23, 4.179, 4.181, 4.182.

**Topography of the small intestine.**

| Segment | Where it lies |
|---|---|
| Duodenum | Epigastric and umbilical regions, above the umbilicus; a C-curve around the head of the pancreas at LI–LIII |
| Jejunum | Mostly in the left upper quadrant: left umbilical, left lumbar and left upper regions |
| Ileum | Mostly in the right lower quadrant (umbilical, hypogastric and right iliac regions), with loops dipping into the pelvis |

The whole coil is framed by the colon (Gray's 4.88) and covered by the greater omentum (Gray's 4.59). Figure: Gray's 4.2 shows the loops in situ under the costal margin and within the colonic frame.

**Peritoneal status.**
- **Intraperitoneal:** the jejunum, the ileum and the first 2–3 cm of the duodenum (the ampulla or cap) are suspended by mesentery. The cap is attached to the liver by the hepatoduodenal ligament (lesser omentum).
- **Secondarily retroperitoneal:** the rest of the duodenum. Its dorsal mesentery fused with the posterior wall during development.

Figures: Gray's 4.6, 4.7, 4.53, 4.54, 4.60.

**Length and calibre.** About 6–7 m from the pyloric orifice to the ileocecal fold. The diameter narrows from beginning to end (Gray's).

**Embryology (for orientation).**
- The duodenum is derived from the foregut and midgut. The junction lies just distal to the major duodenal papilla.
- The jejunum and ileum come from the midgut.
- The midgut loop herniates, rotates 270° counter-clockwise around the SMA, and returns. This explains:
  - the oblique root of the mesentery;
  - the course of the SMA;
  - malrotation and volvulus.

Figures: Gray's 4.13, 4.93, 4.122.

## 1. Duodenum

**Definition.** The first, shortest, widest and most fixed part of the small intestine. It is a C-shaped tube about 20–25 cm long ("twelve finger-breadths"). It runs from the **pyloric orifice** to the **duodenojejunal flexure**, wrapped around the head of the pancreas. It lies above the umbilicus, at the level of vertebrae LI–LIII.

**Parts, levels and relations** (Gray's 4.66, 4.107, 4.140, 4.150):

| Part | Length and level | Anterior | Posterior | Medial | Lateral / other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st, superior** | ~5 cm; to the right of LI; from the pylorus to the neck of the gallbladder. The first 2 cm (ampulla or cap) is intraperitoneal and mobile | Liver (quadrate lobe), gallbladder | Bile duct, gastroduodenal artery, portal vein, IVC | – | Superior: epiploic (omental) foramen. Inferior: head of pancreas |
| **2nd, descending** | ~7–10 cm; from LI to the lower border of LIII, right of the midline | Transverse colon and its mesocolon, liver, jejunal loops | Right kidney (hilum), renal vessels, right ureter, psoas | Head of pancreas; bile and pancreatic ducts enter here | Right colic flexure. Lumen: **major duodenal papilla** (ampulla of Vater; hepatopancreatic sphincter of Oddi) and **minor papilla** (accessory duct) |
| **3rd, inferior (horizontal)** | ~10 cm, the longest; at LIII; runs right to left | **SMA and SMV**, root of the mesentery, jejunal loops | Right ureter, right psoas, right gonadal vessels, **IVC, aorta** and vertebral column | – | Superior: head and uncinate process of the pancreas. Inferior: jejunum |
| **4th, ascending** | ~2.5–5 cm; rises on or left of the aorta to LII | Root of the mesentery, jejunum | Left psoas, left sympathetic trunk, left renal and gonadal vessels, inferior mesenteric vein | Head of pancreas, aorta | Ends at the **duodenojejunal flexure**, held by the **suspensory muscle (ligament of Treitz)** from the right crus |

**Internal features.**
- **Cap (ampulla):** smooth-walled, with no plicae. Seen on barium as the "duodenal cap" (Gray's 4.65).
- **2nd–4th parts:** well-developed **plicae circulares** (Gray's 4.75 endoscopy).
- On the posteromedial wall of the 2nd part, a longitudinal fold leads to the **major duodenal papilla**, about 7–10 cm from the pylorus. The **minor duodenal papilla** lies about 2 cm above it.
- Openings (Gray's 4.109, 4.111; Junqueira 16-7, 16-19; Guyton 65-12):
  - **bile duct** and **main pancreatic duct** → hepatopancreatic ampulla → major papilla;
  - **accessory pancreatic duct** → minor papilla.

**Arterial supply** (Gray's 4.67, 4.124, 4.126):
- **Foregut part**, proximal to the papilla: **celiac trunk** → common hepatic → **gastroduodenal artery**. Branches:
  - supraduodenal artery;
  - anterior and posterior **superior pancreaticoduodenal** arteries;
  - right gastric and right gastro-omental twigs.
- **Midgut part**, distal: **SMA** → **inferior pancreaticoduodenal artery** (anterior and posterior branches), plus the first jejunal artery.
- The superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries anastomose in **anterior and posterior arcades** on the head of the pancreas. This is a celiac–SMA collateral.

**Venous drainage.** Pancreaticoduodenal veins drain to the **SMV** and directly to the **portal vein**. The pre-pyloric vein (of Mayo) marks the pylorus. Figures: Gray's 4.131, 4.132.

**Lymph.** Pancreaticoduodenal nodes → **celiac** and **superior mesenteric** pre-aortic nodes. Figure: Gray's 4.134.

**Nerves.**
- **Sympathetic:** greater and lesser splanchnic nerves (T5–T11) → celiac and superior mesenteric ganglia → periarterial plexuses.
- **Parasympathetic:** vagal trunks.
- **Visceral afferents:** pain is referred to the **epigastric region** (foregut) or **umbilical region** (midgut).

Figures: Gray's 4.20, 4.137, 4.138; Table 4.5.

**Histology.**
- Villi are broad and leaf-like.
- **Duodenal (Brunner) glands** in the submucosa are compound tubular mucous glands. Their alkaline mucus neutralises chyme (Junqueira 15-29; Guyton daily secretions ≈ 200 mL/day).
- The serosa covers only the cap. The rest has an **adventitia**.

**Clinical anatomy.**
- **Duodenal ulcers** occur in the cap. Posterior ulcers erode the gastroduodenal or posterior superior pancreaticoduodenal artery and bleed. Anterior ulcers perforate and cause free gas under the diaphragm (Gray's; Guyton 67-1).
- In **SMA syndrome**, the 3rd part is compressed in the aorto-mesenteric angle.
- Annular pancreas.

## 2. Jejunum and ileum (the mesenteric small intestine)

**Definition.** These are the mobile, intraperitoneal parts of the small intestine, suspended by the mesentery. They run from the **duodenojejunal flexure** (LII, left of the midline) to the **ileocecal junction** (right iliac fossa).
- **Jejunum:** the proximal **two-fifths**, about 2.5 m.
- **Ileum:** the distal **three-fifths**, about 3.5 m.

There is no sharp border; the character changes gradually.

**Location.**
- Jejunum: mostly left upper quadrant.
- Ileum: mostly right lower quadrant and pelvis.
- Both lie within the colonic frame, behind the greater omentum and in front of the posterior abdominal wall structures (kidneys, great vessels, psoas).

Figures: Gray's 4.2, 4.68, 4.181.

**Distinguishing features** (Gray's 4.68, 4.69; Junqueira 15-23):

| Feature | Jejunum | Ileum |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Left upper abdomen | Right lower abdomen, pelvis |
| Wall and diameter | Thicker, wider (~2–4 cm) | Thinner, narrower (~2–3 cm) |
| Colour | Deeper red (more vascular) | Paler pink |
| Plicae circulares | Tall, numerous, closely packed (feathery on barium) | Low, sparse, absent distally |
| Vasa recta | **Long** | **Short** |
| Arterial arcades | **Few** (1–2), large loops | **Many** (up to 4–5), short loops |
| Mesenteric fat | Less, with clear "windows" near the gut | More, reaching to the wall |
| Lymphoid tissue | Scattered nodules | **Peyer's patches** on the antimesenteric wall |
| Villi | Tall, finger-like | Shorter |
| Specialised absorption | Most nutrients, iron and calcium in the duodenum and jejunum | **Vitamin B12–intrinsic factor and bile salts** in the terminal ileum |

**Mesentery** (Gray's 4.61):
- **Shape:** a fan-shaped double fold of peritoneum.
- **Root:** about 15 cm long. It runs obliquely from the **left side of LII** (duodenojejunal flexure) down to the **right sacro-iliac joint** (ileocecal junction). On the way it crosses, in order:
  - the 3rd part of the duodenum;
  - the aorta;
  - the IVC;
  - the right ureter;
  - the right psoas;
  - the right gonadal vessels.
- **Intestinal border:** about 6 m long, which explains the pleating.
- **Contents:** jejunal and ileal vessels, about 100–200 mesenteric lymph nodes, autonomic plexuses, lacteals and fat.

**Arteries** (Gray's 4.126, 4.127; Guyton 63-7):
- The **SMA** arises from the aorta about 1 cm below the celiac trunk, **anterior to LI**.
  - It passes behind the neck of the pancreas and splenic vein.
  - It crosses in front of the left renal vein, the uncinate process and the 3rd part of the duodenum.
  - It then enters the root of the mesentery.
- Branches, in order:
  1. Inferior pancreaticoduodenal.
  2. **15–18 jejunal and ileal arteries from its left side**. These form **arcades** and end in **vasa recta**, which are end arteries alternating to opposite sides of the gut wall.
  3. From the right side: middle colic, right colic and **ileocolic** arteries. The ileocolic supplies the terminal ileum, cecum and appendix.
- **Villus microcirculation:** a central arteriole forms a capillary fountain to the tip, drained by venules. The counter-current arrangement explains villus-tip hypoxia (Guyton 63-8; Junqueira 15-28).

**Veins.**
- Jejunal and ileal veins → **SMV**. The SMV ascends to the right of the SMA in the mesentery, crosses the 3rd part of the duodenum and the uncinate process, and joins the **splenic vein** behind the neck of the pancreas (LII) to form the **portal vein** → liver.
- So absorbed sugars and amino acids reach the liver first.

Figures: Gray's 4.19, 4.131, 4.132; Guyton 63-6.

**Lymph.** The pathway runs:
1. Villus **lacteals** (chylomicrons).
2. Submucosal plexus.
3. Juxta-intestinal and mesenteric nodes.
4. Central **superior mesenteric nodes**.
5. **Intestinal trunk**.
6. **Cisterna chyli** (LI–LII, right of the aorta).
7. **Thoracic duct**.
8. Left venous angle (internal jugular / subclavian junction).

Terminal ileum lymph goes to the **ileocolic nodes**.

Figures: Gray's 4.134, 4.168; Junqueira 15-25, 15-28.

**Nerves** (Gray's 4.136–4.139, 4.171, Table 4.5; Guyton 61-1–61-3, 63-4):

| Supply | Pathway | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Sympathetic | Preganglionic fibres from **T8–T10/11** via the **lesser splanchnic nerve** → synapse in the **superior mesenteric ganglion** → postganglionic fibres ride the SMA branches (periarterial plexus) | Inhibits motility and secretion; vasoconstriction |
| Parasympathetic | **Posterior vagal trunk** via the celiac and superior mesenteric plexuses (preganglionic) → synapse on **enteric neurons** in the wall | Increases motility and secretion |
| Visceral afferents | With the sympathetic fibres to **T9–T10**. Poorly localised midgut pain is referred to the **umbilical region (T10 dermatome)** | — |
| Intrinsic | **Myenteric (Auerbach) plexus** between the muscle layers controls motility. **Submucosal (Meissner) plexus** controls secretion, absorption and local blood flow | — |

**Histology** (Junqueira 15-22 to 15-30; Guyton 66-5 to 66-7):
- **Mucosa.** Three surface amplifications:
  - plicae (×3);
  - villi, 0.5–1.5 mm (×10);
  - microvilli (×20).
- **Epithelium.** Simple columnar, with:
  - **enterocytes** with a brush border and glycocalyx enzymes;
  - **goblet cells**, increasing distally;
  - **Paneth cells** at the crypt base (lysozyme, defensins);
  - **enteroendocrine cells** (I = CCK, S = secretin, K = GIP, Mo = motilin; Junqueira Table 15-1);
  - stem cells;
  - **M cells** over Peyer's patches.
- **Lamina propria:** contains the lacteal, a capillary network, smooth-muscle strands from the muscularis mucosae, plasma cells (IgA) and lymphoid nodules.
- **Submucosa:** contains Brunner glands in the duodenum only, Peyer's patches extending in from the ileum, and the Meissner plexus.
- **Muscularis externa:** inner circular and outer longitudinal layers, with the Auerbach plexus between them (Junqueira 15-30; Guyton 63-2).
- **Serosa:** mesothelium.

**Clinical anatomy.**
- **Meckel's diverticulum.** A remnant of the vitello-intestinal duct on the antimesenteric ileum. The "rule of 2s": about 2% of people, about 2 ft from the ileocecal valve, about 2 in long, often with ectopic gastric mucosa (Gray's 4.78).
- Small bowel obstruction shows dilated loops with valvulae conniventes on X-ray (Gray's 4.95, 4.96; Guyton 67-3).
- Mesenteric ischaemia (SMA embolus).
- Volvulus about the mesentery.

## 3. Ileocecal junction (valve)

**Definition.** The junction where the terminal ileum opens into the **posteromedial wall of the large intestine**, at the junction of the cecum and ascending colon. It lies in the **right iliac fossa**, near the upper border of the right sacro-iliac joint, at the lower end of the root of the mesentery.

**Structure.**
- Two **lips (flaps)**, upper and lower, form the **ileocecal fold**. They project into the cecum and meet at **frenula**.
- Circular muscle of the ileum continues into the flaps, forming a physiological **ileocecal sphincter** (Gray's 4.70, 4.82; Junqueira 15-31).

**Functions** (Guyton 64-4):
- Prevents cecal backflow; the valve resists 50–60 cmH₂O.
- Slows ileal emptying. The sphincter is mildly contracted and relaxes after meals (gastroileal reflex, gastrin).
- Feedback:
  - pressure or irritation in the **cecum** inhibits ileal peristalsis and **contracts** the sphincter;
  - pressure in the **ileum** relaxes it.

**Blood, lymph and nerves.**
- Blood: **ileocolic artery and vein** (SMA, SMV).
- Lymph: ileocolic nodes → superior mesenteric nodes.
- Nerves: superior mesenteric plexus (T10–T11) and vagus. Pain is referred to the umbilical region.

Figures: Gray's 4.85, 4.127.

## 4. Supporting structures that shape small-intestine physiology

**Pancreas and biliary tree.**
- The head of the pancreas lies in the duodenal C.
- The main pancreatic duct joins the bile duct at the hepatopancreatic ampulla → major papilla, guarded by the sphincter of Oddi.
- Secretions:
  - **secretin** (S cells; response to acid) → bicarbonate-rich ductal fluid;
  - **CCK** (I cells; response to fat and protein) → enzyme secretion, gallbladder contraction and relaxation of Oddi.

Figures: Gray's 4.107, 4.109, 4.111; Junqueira 16-7, 16-9, 16-19; Guyton 65-8 to 65-12.

**Stomach–duodenum interface.**
- The pyloric sphincter is a thickened ring of circular muscle at the transpyloric plane, just right of the midline (Gray's 4.64; Guyton 64-2).
- Duodenal feedback via the enterogastric reflex, CCK, secretin and GIP slows gastric emptying.

**Portal vs lymphatic absorption routes.**
- Portal vein → liver: water-soluble nutrients.
- Lacteal → thoracic duct → systemic veins: chylomicrons.

Figures: Gray's 4.19, 4.168; Guyton 63-6.

## 5. How the anatomy explains the physiology (narration anchors)

- **Fixed duodenum, mobile jejunum and ileum.** Retroperitoneal fixation of the duodenum keeps the papilla in a constant place for bile and pancreatic juice. The long mesentery lets the loops mix and slide during segmentation.
- **Wall design.**
  - The circular layer produces segmentation and the contraction ring of peristalsis.
  - The longitudinal layer shortens the segment.
  - The interstitial cells of Cajal set the slow waves: about 12/min in the duodenum, 8–9/min in the terminal ileum (Guyton 63-3, 8-7).
  - The myenteric plexus coordinates the peristaltic reflex (Guyton 63-4, 63-5, 64-3).
- **Surface amplification** (plicae, villi, microvilli; about 250 m²) underlies absorption (Junqueira 15-22, 4-8; Guyton 66-5 to 66-7).
- **Vascular architecture.** Long vasa recta and capillary loops in the villi give high flow for uptake. Venous return is portal; lymph return is via lacteals (Gray's 4.69; Guyton 63-6, 63-8).
- **Regional specialisation.**
  - Duodenum: Brunner glands neutralise acid, and enzymes and bile mix in.
  - Jejunum: bulk absorption.
  - Ileum: B12 and bile salts (enterohepatic circulation), Peyer's patches, and the valve that controls delivery to the colon.
